abstract: The United Nations Population Division publishes estimates of the international migrant stock by origin and destination, revised every few years. We compare the 2020 and 2024 editions for one origin, Greece, across every destination both editions itemise. The 2024 edition reports $n = 775{,}995$ Greek-born people abroad in 2020 against $1{,}088{,}507$ in the 2020 edition, a revision of $-28.7\%$. The revision is not uniform: Germany and Türkiye are revised down by a third and by nearly two thirds, Australia and Canada by about 15%, while the United States is revised *up* by 15% [@un2024]. Everything on this page, including the data, is public in DNS and can be reproduced with `dig`.

# 1. Background

text: Each edition of the *International Migrant Stock* re-estimates past years as new censuses arrive, so the same year can carry different values in different editions [@un2020]. Users who mix editions get inconsistent series. This note measures the size of the 2020-to-2024 revision for a single origin with a long emigration history [@kasimis].
text: Let $S_{d,y}^{(e)}$ be the stock of Greek-born people in destination $d$ in year $y$ as reported by edition $e$. We define the revision ratio

math: r_{d,y} = \frac{S_{d,y}^{(2024)}}{S_{d,y}^{(2020)}}
  label: 1

text: and report it for $y = 2020$, the latest year both editions cover.[^Both editions give mid-year estimates. The 2024 edition itemises only the largest origins per destination, so destinations where Greece falls into its "Others" row are absent from the comparison; see Section 4.]

# 2. Data

text: The site's database holds both editions as tables (`stock_rev2020` and `stock`). The comparison set is the destinations present in both for 2020.

number:
  query: SELECT COUNT(*) AS value, 'destination countries present in both editions for 2020' AS label FROM stock a JOIN stock_rev2020 b ON a.iso2=b.iso2 AND a.year=b.year WHERE a.year=2020

text: [@f5] makes the pattern concrete: the United States series is revised up across the whole period (134,752 against 116,619 for 2020, $r = 1.16$), while Germany's 2020 value falls from 391,502 to 248,036, $r = 0.63$. Türkiye, not shown, has the largest revision of any major destination, $r = 0.37$.

# 4. Limitations

text: The 2024 edition folds smaller origins into an "Others" row for each destination, so the comparison excludes destinations where Greece was itemised in 2020 but not in 2024, including the United Kingdom and France.[^In the 2020 edition the United Kingdom held 75,441 Greek-born residents, the fifth-largest community; its absence from the 2024 edition is a reporting change, not a population change.] The revision therefore mixes methodological change with coverage change, and this note does not separate them.

# 5. Conclusion

text: Mixing editions of the migrant-stock tables is unsafe for Greece: the 2024 edition revises the 2020 total down by 28.7%, by more than that for Germany and Türkiye, and in the opposite direction for the United States. Analyses should use one edition throughout and state which, as [@f1] through [@f5] do here.
