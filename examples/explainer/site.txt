text: Every website you have ever visited lived on a computer somewhere. Someone paid for it, patched it, and will one day stop. DNSite is an experiment in removing that computer. The site is stored in the one piece of infrastructure a domain already has, its DNS records, and assembled in your browser from there. This page is one. Run `dig TXT _dnsite.dnsite.org` and you are reading its source.

text: DNS can hold short pieces of text against a name. DNSite writes a site into those pieces: a readable document in a tiny site language, plus an optional SQLite database and vector images, compressed and split across records named `s0`, `s1`, `d0` and so on, with a manifest at `_dnsite` that says how many. Any domain can point at one shared, static **loader** page. When you open the domain, that page asks DNS for the domain's own records and draws what it finds ([@how]).

# What it is good for

chart: table
  title: Fits and doesn't
  query: SELECT kind AS "Site", CASE fits WHEN 1 THEN 'yes' ELSE 'no' END AS "Fits?", why AS "Because" FROM fits
text: The limits are honest ones. DNS hosts cap a record at a few kilobytes and a zone at a few hundred to a few thousand records, so a site of a few hundred kilobytes is the practical ceiling. Text is nearly free; a photo costs about as much as seven blog posts. And only the domain's owner can change DNS, so a DNSite is read-only for visitors, which is exactly right for the things people publish rather than collect.[^Comments, sign-ups and orders need a server somewhere. That server can be anyone's; the site itself stays in DNS.]

# How to make one

text: **1. Write it.** Open [dnsite.org/make](https://dnsite.org/make.html). Add blocks from the list: headings, paragraphs, posts, charts, images, embeds, equations. The preview on the right is the real site.
text: **2. Add data, if you have any.** Drop a CSV on the Data tab and it becomes a table. Charts pick columns from dropdowns; no SQL required, though it is there if you want it.
text: **3. Add images.** SVGs go in as they are, cleaned and made safe. Photos are shrunk to WebP in your browser. Big pictures can stay on a host you already have and be linked by URL.
text: **4. Publish.** Type your domain, download the zone file, and import it at your DNS host, or paste the records. Point the domain at a loader with a CNAME. Press *Check the domain* to watch the records land.
text: **5. Change it whenever.** Edit, publish again, done. The old version expires out of the world's caches within minutes.
text: There is nothing to install, no account to make, and no server to keep alive. If dnsite.org itself ever disappears, the loader is [open source](https://github.com/chucklit/dnsite): point your domain at another copy and your site is back.
