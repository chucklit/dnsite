post: Inside a pumpkin
  date: 2026-10-24
text: Cut one in half and you are looking at a **berry**. Botanically a pumpkin is a *pepo*: a fruit with a hard rind and a fleshy interior, in the same family as cucumbers, melons and gourds. Everything you can see has a job.
image: cross_section
  alt: A labelled cross-section of a pumpkin showing the stem, rind, flesh, seed cavity, seeds, fibrous strands, ribs and blossom end
  caption: A pumpkin in section. Drawn, not photographed, so the whole thing is a few kilobytes.
  width: 100%
  align: full
  figure: cut
text: **Stem (peduncle).** The handle. Its woody base is the pumpkin's weak point; a snapped stem is where rot gets in, which is why growers cut with pruners and leave a few inches.
text: **Rind.** The skin, hardened by curing in the September sun. Its ridges follow the **ribs**, the vascular lines that fed the fruit as it grew; count them and you usually get a number between eight and fourteen.
text: **Flesh (mesocarp).** The orange wall, usually an inch or two thick, and the only part a pie wants. Pie varieties grow a thick, dry, sweet wall; carving varieties grow a thin, watery one so the fruit can get big.
text: **Seed cavity.** The hollow middle, laced with the **fibrous strands** everyone scrapes out at Halloween. Those strands are the placental tissue that fed the seeds.
text: **Seeds.** A carving pumpkin carries a few hundred, each one a flat, oval, oil-rich package that is also the easiest part to eat.[^Roast them at 350 °F for 15 to 20 minutes with salt and oil. Hulless varieties, bred in Styria for oil, skip the shell entirely.]
text: **Blossom end.** The scar opposite the stem, where the flower was. If it is soft, the pumpkin was picked wet or stored badly.
quote: A pumpkin is a berry with ambition.
  by: a thing I say to customers who ask

post: The harvest, scheduled
  date: 2026-10-17
text: Pumpkins are a long crop for a short market. Most of what is grown in the United States is sold in the five weeks before Halloween, and most of what is canned is grown within a day's drive of Morton, Illinois.

chart: hbars

text: [@states] is the reason the canning crop and the carving crop are different businesses. Illinois grows for the cannery on contract; everyone else grows for the roadside stand and the grocery display, where the pumpkin is sold by the look of it.

post: Why pumpkin spice comes early and leaves before Halloween
  date: 2026-10-10
text: The pumpkins in the field turn orange in late September. The pumpkin-flavoured world does not wait for them. Starbucks' Pumpkin Spice Latte, the bellwether, has crept from a 10 October debut in 2003 to the fourth week of August. Pumpkin beers ship in August so they are fully stocked by September, and by Halloween the shelf has already turned to Oktoberfest and winter ales.
chart: line
  title: The day the Pumpkin Spice Latte returned, 2011–2025 (day of the year)
  query: SELECT year AS x, doy AS y FROM psl WHERE year >= 2011
  annotate: x=2015 | latest, 4 Sept; x=2024 | earliest, 22 Aug
  ymin: 225
  ymax: 255
  format: ,
  figure: psl
  note: Day 244 is 1 September. Sources: Starbucks announcements and press reports, 2011–2025. The first test, in 2003, began on day 283 (10 October).
chart: table
  title: PSL return dates
  query: SELECT year AS "Year", month || '/' || day AS "Date", (304 - doy) AS "Days before Halloween", note AS "Note" FROM psl ORDER BY year
  figure: psldates
text: [@psl] shows the creep: about a week earlier over fifteen years, with 2024's 22 August the earliest yet, ten weeks before the night it supposedly celebrates. The logic is retail, not horticulture. A seasonal product earns for as long as it is on the shelf, and the shelf is rented from the previous season. Pumpkin creeps into August; Oktoberfest and holiday beers creep into October; by 31 October the pumpkin beer is already on clearance.
text: Beer followed the latte. Nielsen counted 53 pumpkin beers on the market in 2012 and 65 the next year; brewers started shipping in August to be fully distributed by September; and the category's grocery-store volume peaked in 2015 at roughly 8,800 barrels before Oktoberfest styles took its place as the top fall seasonal.[^Volume figures are IRI scan data for grocery, convenience and drug stores, as reported by Good Beer Hunting in August 2019. They exclude bars and brewery taprooms.]

text: **A Maryland footnote.** Pumpkin spice, the blend, is a Baltimore product. McCormick & Company, founded in Baltimore in 1889, put its pumpkin pie spice on grocery shelves in 1934, five years after Libby's canned pumpkin created the demand. Cinnamon, ginger, nutmeg, allspice; no pumpkin. The company still makes it, from Hunt Valley, Maryland, about twenty minutes up the road from this notebook.
